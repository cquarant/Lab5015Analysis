Does the choice of REF bar change the measured resolution?

Each DUT bar has a left and a right timestamp. Their difference measures timing fluctuations without using the REF timestamp. A second approach measures each end against an external crystal. We test whether selecting different external bars changes the agreement between the two approaches.

Four runs cover 16 DUT bars, six T1 thresholds and eight bars of the DM0348 reference module. The main comparison aligns the time offsets of the eight REF samples and compares their union with REF7 alone.

Compare the centered union with REF7 →

What the comparison shows

For the central DUT bars (4–11) at T1 = 10, the median Gaussian mixed-minus-direct gap in the centered union ranges from −1.64 to −0.07 ps across the four runs. At T1 = 3, run 4281 retains a larger gap with both selections: +13.31 ps with the centered union and +14.42 ps with REF7.

These point estimates give little support to REF choice alone explaining the discrepancy. The timing methods also differ in their corrections and in how their fitted widths enter the calculation.
